**Plain-language summary of closures affecting the crew:**
- **Beech Mountain Loop (east section):** closed below the summit/fire tower. Use the outer loop, Beech South Ridge, or Beech West Ridge trails instead.
- **Schoodic Mountain Road:** closed from Schoodic Loop Road to Schoodic Head summit. If we do Schoodic, plan on hiking rather than driving to the summit.
- **Ocean Path (south section):** closed from the trailhead across from Otter Point parking to the southern terminus. **This one matters — Ocean Path is one of Acadia's signature hikes (Sand Beach → Thunder Hole → Otter Cliff). The lower/southern portion near Otter Point is off-limits; the Sand Beach/Thunder Hole end remains accessible.** The park's Current Conditions page also documents storm-damage history and temporary repairs at Ocean Path from the January 2024 storms — check current status before committing to a full-length route.
- No alert-level closures for the Park Loop Road itself, the carriage roads, or the main visitor facilities as of this digest.

### Planning notes from the park home page (nps.gov/acad)

- **Entrance pass required year-round.** Vehicles must display a pass clearly visible through the windshield. Buy and print a pass online in advance.
- **Vehicle reservations are required to drive Cadillac Summit Road from late May to mid-October.** Sold online only (Recreation.gov) — **not sold at the park.** The entrance pass is separate. If our trip lands after mid-October, this requirement has ended for the season (confirm the park's current cut-off).
- **Camping takes planning:** all campsite reservations must be made online in advance. **No backcountry camping and no overnight parking** anywhere in Acadia.
- **Park size/scope:** 27 miles of historic motor roads, 158 miles of hiking trails, 45 miles of carriage roads; ~4 million visits a year (top-10 most-visited US national park).
- **Wabanaki Nations:** the park highlights the heritage, culture, and continuing presence of the tribes known collectively as Wabanaki, "People of the Dawnland."
- **Off-season note:** the park actively promotes November–April visits — plenty to do outside peak season.
- **Accessibility:** Acadia is committed to accessible facilities, landscapes, programs, and information.
- **Other home-page pointers:** rules on pets ("Bark Ranger" / protect your pet privileges), park rules & regulations, special events/research/climbing permits, invasive species, and leave your firewood at home (don't transport firewood).

### Current Conditions page notes (nps.gov/acad/planyourvisit/conditions.htm)

- **Beaches:** Sand Beach, Echo Lake Beach, and Lake Wood are monitored weekly for bacteria **June through August only** — monitoring has ended for the season.
- **Weather:** be prepared for quick changes from warm and sunny to cold and rainy; weather on Cadillac Mountain and Acadia's other peaks can be drastically different from Bar Harbor.
- **Winter storms (January 2024) damage history:** significant storm damage across the park — Ocean Path sections (temporary repairs completed with Friends of Acadia support), Sand Beach railing/staircase (stabilized), significant dune and bluff erosion, Little Hunters Beach lower steps washed out (temporary replacement stairs built), Otter Cove causeway riprap damage and destroyed trail section, Seawall Picnic Area and Seawall Campground downed/hazard trees, debris cleanup on Thompson Island, Park Loop Road, and Schoodic Loop Road, Blue Duck Ships Store foundation undermined (repaired by lessee), and Rockefeller Hall roof/water damage. NPS staff continue responding to long-term impacts — **exercise caution while exploring.**
- **Schoodic Loop Road:** ~200 feet of the right lane was undermined by storms; the road remains passable and open.
- **Roads/parking/trails detail lists:** the Current Conditions page loads facility-specific lists dynamically (best viewed in a browser); the **Operating Hours & Seasons** page is where the park publishes detailed facility opening/closing dates — check it before finalizing our dates.
- **Park contact:** PO Box 177, Bar Harbor, ME 04609 · 207-288-3338 (TTY 207-288-8800) · acadia_information@nps.gov

## 3. Web Search Findings

The web search leg returned genuinely useful, current coverage. Highlights worth folding into our planning:

### Fall foliage timing (worth knowing even if we're late)
- **Peak color at Acadia this season was around Oct 12, with the best window Oct 11–14** per the More Than Just Parks foliage tracker (updated Oct 19). As of Oct 20 the park is listed as **just past peak**. Higher elevations peaked around Oct 12; low ground around Oct 13. (https://morethanjustparks.com/foliage-tracker/maine/acadia-national-park)
- The tracker noted this season's ground reports moved the peak **2 days earlier** than the model alone would predict. Roadsides and wetland edges turn first; woods behind them run a few days later.
- Other guides (nationalparktrips.com, lifecircu.com) put typical peak in the **first two weeks of October**, with coastal Mount Desert Island peaking later than inland Maine and color sometimes lingering into early November. For a late-October/November visit, expect past-peak but still-scenic conditions and thinner crowds.

### Cadillac Summit Road reservation mechanics (if anyone wants sunrise)
- Reservations run **May 20 – Oct 25, 2026, $6/vehicle via Recreation.gov** (lifecircu.com fall guide).
- **30% of reservations release 90 days ahead at 10 a.m. ET; the remaining 70% release two days before, also at 10 a.m.** That second release is the detail most guides skip — a missed 90-day window isn't the end of the trip.
- No departure time once inside (stay until the road closes in the evening). One sunrise reservation per vehicle per 7 days; one daytime reservation per day. **Cell service is unreliable — save the QR code confirmation before arriving.**

### Crowds and the new Gateway Center
- **Acadia logged over 4 million visitors in 2025 — an all-time record.** October weekends during peak foliage now rival the busiest days of July (lifecircu.com).
- **Cruise ships still call in autumn** — Bar Harbor crowds don't vanish with summer.
- **The new Acadia Gateway Center in Trenton (opened May 2026) offers 300 free parking spaces just off the island, with shuttle service** — a real alternative to circling for a spot at Sand Beach or the Beehive trailhead.
- **Arriving before 8 a.m. or after 3 p.m.** remains the most reliable way to beat traffic and tour buses.

### Trail intel from recent write-ups
- **Beehive Trail:** ~1.4–1.5 miles, ~500 ft of gain; trailhead across from Sand Beach parking; **hike it counterclockwise** so you ascend the iron rungs rather than descend them.
- **Precipice Trail:** ~2.1 miles, 1,000+ ft of climbing, runs clockwise, steep drop-offs — the more serious of the two iron-rung routes.
- Fee write-ups across sources **cross-check cleanly with NPS data** ($35 vehicle / $20 person / $30 motorcycle / $70 Acadia annual / $80 America the Beautiful / $100 nonresident surcharge / $6 Cadillac reservation).
